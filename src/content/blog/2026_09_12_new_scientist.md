What I learnt from New Scientist magazine, issue date September 12, 2026

## Lithium could protect against Alzheimer's

Alice Klein [reports](https://www.newscientist.com/article/2586894-should-we-all-be-taking-low-doses-of-lithium-to-prevent-alzheimers/) that studies have found that in counties with higher levels of lithium in public water dementia levels are lower than where lithium levels are lower.

Trials are starting on the use of lithium orotate supplements as a preventive treatment for dementia.

Too much lithium can have serious health impacts and so supplements are not currently recommended.

## Nuclear reactors underground

Deep Fusion, a California company is preparing to construct a 9m high by less than 1m diameter high pressure reactor that will be 1,600m underground.  The advantage of deep installation is the minimal need for containment.  Output is planned to be 15MW.

As discussed in the [article](https://www.newscientist.com/article/2587049-a-nuclear-reactor-is-being-installed-a-mile-underground-in-kansas/), there is potential for rapid roll-out but there are questions over maintenance and long term safety.

## Laughter Therapy

Laughter therapy is [reported](https://www.newscientist.com/article/2587748-laughter-therapy-may-help-people-with-chronic-lung-disease/) to be beneficial in treating patients with chronic pulmonary disease.  Could we all benefit from finding more ways to have a laugh?  I'd be a willing volunteer!
