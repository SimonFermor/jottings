A useful relaxation technique is described in a YouTube [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u0OBgihk2f8) with Andrew Huberman.  He explains how it's hard to practice top-down mechanisms such as mindfulness, meditation, intention, gratitude and other techniques in the moment when one is stressed and tired.

He describes the physiological sigh as a breathing technique that is the fastest way to use the autonomic nerve system to feel more relaxed when stressed.

When stressed the heart automatically beats faster.  Although the heart can't be controlled directly it is possible to use physiological sighs to indirectly lower the heart rate.

When inhaling the diaphragm moves down, the heart expands and blood circulation slows down.  The sinal atrial node neurons then sends a signal to the brain and the heart speeds up to compensate.

When exhaling, the opposite happens and the heart will slow down.

This is the physiological sigh and occurs automatically in certain situations, such as when going to sleep to starting to cry.

Breathing in and then exhaling slowly helps reduce the heart rate.  Breathing in and then taking a sharp further inhale before slowly exhaling produces the physiological sigh and is more effective.

Carbon dioxide builds up when stressed.  The long exhale is more effective at expelling carbon dioxide and this helps with relaxation.

Stress can't be directly controlled by the mind.  Repeating the physiological sigh two or three times is the most effective way to manage stress in the moment.
