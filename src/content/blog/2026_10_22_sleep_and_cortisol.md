Cortisol is a steroid hormone that helps in the bodies response to inflammnation and situations triggering a fight or flight response.  Poor sleep can lead to high levels of cortisol.  The Cleveland Clinic has a useful [article](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/22187-cortisol) on cortisol and includes the following suggestions for reducing cortisol levels:

- laugh (find humor through the day)
- sleep well
- physical activity
- practice deep breathing
- maintain healthy relationships

Eating close to bed-time can produce elevated cortisol levels in the night, which can lead to waking in the middle of the night and having difficulty getting back to sleep.
