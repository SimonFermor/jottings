## What is worry management?

- thinking about worries differently
- finding ways to live so that worries don't adversely affect sleep
- becoming better able to manage and reduce the impact of anxiety and rumination

Worries can activate the flight or fight response, which increases heart rate, sweating etc.

Worries about sleep can make it harder to sleep!

## Worry writing

Write down worrisome thoughts and for each thought:

1. What is a less bad way of looking at this?
2. What is the actual likelihood of this happening?
3. What evidence do I have for these worries?
4. What evidence do I have against these worries?
5. How have I coped with similar situations in the past?

Don't try to write down details for every worry at once.  One or two worries a day is enough, so that each worry can be fully explored.  Other worries can be listed and considered in a day or two.

If a worry is hard to write about it might be necessary to talk to a therapist or counselor to do this in a safe and supportive environment.

For worries where there can be action, write down the next action and when it will be done.  For other worries, practice acceptance and acknowledge that the worry can't be solved.

Finish by practicing gratitude to encourage a calm state of mind.

## Timing

When to practice worry management

- at least an hour before bed

Suggestion: Set aside 15 minutes in the day for worries

## Benefits

By writing down worries in a scheduled worry time the brain is re-trained to accept that there is no need to worry in the night as worries are being taken care of.

## Waking In The Night

If worries still keep you awake in the night, get up and write down the list of what is worrying and then go back to bed.

## Acceptance

It's important to accept that there is much that can't be controlled.  There is much uncertainty in life and action can be taken on what can be controlled.  What can't be controlled or influenced needs to be accepted.
