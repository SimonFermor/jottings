Cognitive behaviour therapy supported by medication has been the conventional approach to treating depression.  An [article](https://www.newscientist.com/article/2590434-lifestyle-changes-may-be-as-effective-as-therapy-at-easing-depression/) in the [New Scientist](https://www.newscientist.com/) covers recent research that shows that Lifestyle changes can also be effective in treating depression.

A couple of quotes from the [article](https://www.newscientist.com/article/2590434-lifestyle-changes-may-be-as-effective-as-therapy-at-easing-depression/):

The recommended lifestyle changes are:

- moderate alcohol intake
- not smoking
- getting enough sleep
- regularly exercising
- eating a healthy diet
- frequently socialising
- minimising sedentary behaviour

_On average, the lifestyle intervention led to an 8.55-point decrease, 1.73 points more than the 6.82-point decrease in the CBT group._

_[Ashwini Nadkarni](https://connects.catalyst.harvard.edu/Profiles/display/Person/124779) at Harvard Medical School. “Changes in one’s lifestyle can impact depression through several pathways, including inflammation, stress and neuroplasticity, which is the reorganisation of structure, function and connections in the brain._
