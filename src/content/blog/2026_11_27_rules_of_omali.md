## Rules of the Game

### The objective of the game

Omali is a game for two players in which each of you is trying to capture more of the 36 stones on the board than your opponent.

### Setting up the game

The two sides of the Omali board are clipped together by means of the special clip provided, so that you will be playing on a board consisting of six holes (or "cells") on both sides, with a large cell (or "Omali") at each end.

You sit facing one another either side of the board.

The six cells nearest you belong to you, and the six cells opposite to your opponent. Equally, the large cell at the end of the board to your right is your Omali, and the one at the opposite end belongs to your opponent.

The 36 counters or "stones" in the game are placed around the board, 3 in each of the 12 cells. The Omali cells are left vacant.

The game is now ready for play.

### How the game is played

Having decided who goes first, the first player then starts the game by selecting one of the six cells on his or her side of the board and removes from it all the stones in that cell (obviously three at this stage of the game).

You then drop (or "sow") all of these stones, one per cell without missing out any cell, in an anti-clockwise direction around the board to your right until you run out of stones.

You must always sow one stone per cell, including your own Omali and your opponent's cells if you reach that far. But you never sow stones into your opponent's Omali; in the event that you do have enough stones to sow that far, you merely bypass your opponent's Omali, and come full circle back to your own cells and continue sowing into these.

Play continues alternately, each of you in turn choosing one of your own cells, removing the stones and sowing them around the board as just described. How you score and capture stones is described in the following section.

The game finishes when one of you is unable to sow any more stones since all your six cells are empty.

### How you score and capture

Whenever you are able to sow a stone into your own Omali it becomes yours, and takes no further part in the game.

An additional bonus is provided if you are able to sow the last stone in your hand into your own Omali: you are then given another turn.

You may capture stones on your opponent's side as follows: if the last stone you sow lands in an empty cell on your own side of the board, then that stone, and all the stones in the cell opposite on your opponent's side are removed and placed in your Omali, hence being added to your score.

### The end of the game

The game finishes when one side of the board is completely emptied of stones.

If yours is the side to be emptied first, all the stones on the opposite side of the board become yours and are added to the other stones in your Omali.

Similarly, your opponent may well trap you into letting his side become empty first and thus capture those stones remaining on your side.

The winner of the game is the player with the greatest number of stones (i.e. 19 or more).
